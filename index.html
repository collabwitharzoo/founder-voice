What I do
  • Positioning One sentence that says what you are known for, and the three arguments only you can make. Everything else is downstream of this.
  • Ghostwriting in your voice Posts drafted from a 45-minute call a week. They sound like you talking, not like a LinkedIn post. If a reader can tell it was outsourced, I have failed.
  • Profile as a landing page Headline, banner, About, featured section. The part every profile visit lands on and most founders wrote once in 2019.
  • Distribution Comments, DMs, connection strategy, the founders and operators worth being visible to. Posting without this is writing into a drawer.
  • Measurement The tracker I built for myself, set up on your profile: every post’s reach and engagement in one view, refreshed daily. Reviewed monthly so we cut what only looked like it worked.
How it runs

Four steps, then a weekly rhythm.

  1. Voice audit I read everything you have already published, plus a recorded conversation. I come back with how you actually sound and where the profile is leaking attention. Week 1
  2. Positioning and pillars We agree on the four themes you post inside for the next quarter, and rewrite the profile to match. Week 1–2
  3. Writing rhythm One call a week, three posts drafted, you approve or redline. Scheduled, published, commented under for the first two hours. Ongoing
  4. Review and adjust Monthly read of the tracker. What earned saves and DMs gets more room, what earned polite likes gets cut. Monthly
The pillars

Most founders post one of these and wonder why it flattens.

  • The buildWhat you shipped, what broke, what it cost. The only content competitors cannot copy.
  • The point of viewThe thing about your market you believe and most people do not. This is what gets quoted.
  • The proofCustomers, numbers, outcomes, told as a story rather than an announcement.
  • The personWhy you, why now. The pillar founders skip, and the one that makes strangers reply.
